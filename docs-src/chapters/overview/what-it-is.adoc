== What an Eidolon is

A *digital reflection* is a maintained, AI-readable record of one subject: its identity, context, relationships, evidence, provenance,
history and lifecycle. It is not a replica and not necessarily a digital twin. It is enough grounded evidence for an AI to reason about
the real subject accurately and flexibly. There are three kinds, and they are not interchangeable (specs 0047 and 0048):

[cols="1,2,2"]
|===
|Kind |Its subject |Its central question

|*Eidolon*
|An independently identifiable real-world entity: a person, a company, an institution, a facility, an identifiable physical asset.
|What exists?

|*Ergon*
|A deliberately created operational solution: a product, a service, a system, a workflow, an AI Workforce solution.
|What is built?

|*Noema*
|An abstract intellectual construct: a concept, a hypothesis, a theory, a principle, a framework, a methodology, a research question.
|What is conceived?
|===

The concept of the Eidolon is the founder's own authored research; the system in this repository is the company's first working
instance of it, and the "Eidolon" of the next sections is that system, which holds reflections of all three kinds. The Ergon and the Noema
are its counterparts. A reflection is always the record, never its subject: a Noema kept in a document is still a Noema, and an
Eidolon of a person is not the person's profile page.

=== The problem

Organizations describe themselves in prose: a website, a deck, a one-pager, a founder's memory of what was decided and why. Prose
drifts. A page says the company does five things; by the time a reader, or an AI, encounters it, the company does three of them,
differently, for different reasons. A person can sense that something is stale. An AI usually cannot, and will reason confidently from
the stale version.

An Eidolon inverts the usual order. Instead of writing a description and hoping it stays true, it asserts *facts*: discrete, dated,
sourced, each carrying its own visibility. Descriptions are generated from those facts, or checked against them.

=== Specs, ontology and work

We think the best shape for any AI-native deliverable has three parts, always made in this order:

. *Specs say what must be true.* A spec is a short, numbered, testable statement of intent: "the public root MUST contain only
Public-tier facts." Because each requirement is testable, a person or an AI can check a deliverable against it instead of guessing
what the author meant. Specs live in `spec-kit/specs/`, and <<reference-specs>> lists them.
. *The ontology says what things are.* The ontology is the shared, typed vocabulary: what a Unit, a Work, a Decision, a Right, an
Audience, a Reference is, and how they relate. It reuses established standards (W3C PROV, schema.org, SKOS, Dublin Core) rather than
inventing terms, so an AI does not have to infer what "asset" or "decision" means in this company. It lives in `ontology/`.
. *Work is what gets made.* Books, websites, research records, skills, spoken works, ventures: the deliverables. Each is produced
against the specs and typed against the ontology, so it can be audited. Why does this exist, what rule shaped it, what does it
claim, who may see it.

The order is not a preference; it is a rule (spec 0001, FR-037). A new capability is established in a spec before it is represented in
the ontology, and in the ontology before it is implemented anywhere else. Each layer is only as good as the one before it: an ontology
with no spec is vocabulary without intent, and work with no ontology is prose again, ready to drift.

Why this suits AI in particular:

* *AI needs grounding, not eloquence.* A model briefed from typed facts with declared sources reasons about the company the way a
well-briefed person would. A model briefed from a brochure repeats the brochure.
* *AI needs a stopping rule.* Specs say where the work is done and where human judgment resumes (for example, where AI's role stops in
Research & IP, spec 0006).
* *AI needs to know what it may say.* Every fact declares its audience, so an AI producing a public document cannot silently pull in
something confidential.
* *People need to audit the result.* The test of an Eidolon is whether an AI reasoning from it reaches the conclusion a well-briefed
person would, and whether a person auditing it can find out why. How elegant the ontology looks does not matter.

=== What makes it hold together

* *A vocabulary before any content.* Terms are agreed before facts go in.
* *A confidentiality model that is not a ladder.* Audiences overlap (public, anyone affiliated, a specific agreement, a specific
role) and a fact may belong to several; clearance for one does not imply another.
* *A hard line between what we assert and what we merely watch.* A patent's status belongs to the patent office, not to our own
saying-so. We hold a _reference_ with a verification date and cadence, not a copy of the value.

[#two-kinds]
=== Three kinds of reflection

An Eidolon helps answer: what is this entity, what do we know about it and how do we know, what relationships does it have, what has
changed, what matters now. An Ergon helps answer: why was this made, what problem is it for, how does it work, what does it depend on,
who builds, owns, operates, buys and uses it, what does it promise, what shows that it works. A Noema helps answer: what is the idea,
what motivated it, what does it assume, what supports and contradicts it, what would falsify it, what follows if it is correct, what
could be built or tried from it, and how has our understanding changed. All three carry the same things: a stable identifier, types,
assertions with sources and a label (observation, hypothesis, evidence, inference, recommendation or unknown), relationships with their
dates, an audience, a review state and a steward of the record. A steward of a record does not thereby own, or have authority over,
its subject.

*To decide which a new subject is*, ask four questions of the subject, never of the record, and never "is it physical, digital,
human-made or natural?", which decides nothing:

[cols="1,2,2,2"]
|===
|Question |Eidolon |Ergon |Noema

|Identity
|An individual in the world with its own identity.
|A design, a method or a kind of thing, with versions.
|An idea, defined by what it means.

|Purpose
|Not made for a purpose of the record's owner.
|Deliberately designed or assembled to accomplish one.
|Conceived to explain, predict or guide; it can be false.

|Abstraction
|One particular: this device, this building, this company.
|The design of many particulars: the model, the product.
|A proposition, a construct, a method in the abstract.

|Lifecycle
|Exists before and apart from the record, and is observed.
|Is specified, built, released and evolved.
|Is proposed, investigated, supported or falsified, and revised.
|===

Then find the subject's class in the ontology's classification rules (`agora ontology list --match Rule`): the most specific rule
decides. If none does, or two disagree, the kind is *undetermined*: do not guess. Type the subject more specifically, add a rule
(spec first), or ask a person. A subject that is none of the three, such as an event, a legal right, a patent, a claim or an outcome,
has the kind *other* and no reflection.

The boundary cases are the useful ones:

* A company is an Eidolon even though people formed it. A deployed device unit is an Eidolon; its model, its software and its
intended capabilities are Ergons, joined by `instantiates`.
* A methodology is a Noema. A deployed workflow that carries it out is an Ergon, joined to it by `operationalizes`; each run of the
workflow is an event, not another Ergon. An AI Workforce is an idea (a Noema) and one deployment of it (an Ergon), with the
organizations and people that develop, operate, buy and use the deployment as Eidolons.
* A patent document, the right in it, the invention it describes and the product that practices it are four subjects: a document and a
right (kind other), a Noema, and an Ergon. Who invented, who owns, who was assigned and who may license are four relationships, and
none is inferred from another. Proposing, researching or adopting an idea is not owning it, and nobody owns an idea.

*A claim is not a Noema.* A claim is one statement that something is the case (`schema:Claim`). A Noema is the construct and its
history, and it _states_ claims; the same claim can be stated by several Noemas, and a patent claim belongs to its patent. An
_assertion_ is a third thing: what a record says about its subject, with its label, source and review. Recording that a hypothesis
states a claim does not assert that the claim is true.

*A Noema can be wrong.* Its epistemic state (proposed, under investigation, supported, contested, falsified, superseded) is an
assessment: dated, on a stated basis, and in force only when a person has accepted it. It is not proven, it is not its publication or
approval stage, and a falsified hypothesis stays, with its evidence, its relationships and its history, as a research asset. Evidence
for and against it are separate sourced relationships that sit side by side; nothing nets them off. What the idea assumes, predicts,
would be refuted by, and where it is limited are assertions with an aspect.

*An AI proposes; a person accepts.* An assertion or a record has a review state: candidate, reviewed, accepted or rejected, and none
stated is a candidate. An AI agent's proposal is a candidate until a person reviews it, and only accepted records count toward a
Noema's state or its research stage. Review is not verification: a hypothesis can be accepted as a hypothesis and is never verified.

*Commercial use is one possible consequence, not a requirement.* The path from an observation to a compounding advantage (observation,
conception, investigation, relevance, experimentation, operationalization, validation, compounding) is read from the accepted records
and never stored or demanded. Demand evidence must name the identifiable Eidolons it came from; novelty, citations, patentability and
technical elegance are not demand.

Relationships join subjects, never records, and each has a type, a direction, a source, dates and a label for how settled it is. The
worked examples are in `spec-kit/specs/0047-digital-reflections/examples/` (a customer account, a medical device, an AI Workforce
solution, rights and inventions) and `spec-kit/specs/0048-noemas/examples/` (Native Alpha, Unbundling Work, engineering care delivery,
the AI Workforce idea, and a hypothesis that is falsified). They are illustrative, invented, and checked with the ontology.

Two commands read this for an agent or a person, and call no AI: `agora reflection list` (by kind, epistemic state or review state;
`--review candidate` lists what awaits a person; `--duplicates`; `--audit`) and `agora reflection show` (one subject: its state,
claims, evidence for and against, assumptions, falsifiers, research stage and why it may need another look). An agent that finds a
candidate Noema in a document drafts it as a candidate, attributed to the agent, for a person to review.

*A record typed with the wrong kind* is still served at its identifier. `agora check ontology` (and the private repository's check,
which runs the same rules) reports it as needing review and changes nothing, and `agora reflection list --audit` shows how a whole
repository's subjects classify. A person reviews each one: a new record of the right kind is made that reflects the same subject and is
derived from the old record, and the old record stays and points to it, so identifiers and relationships do not change. A subject no one
can decide stays as it is and goes on the repository's register of open questions. Changing the rules and moving records are separate
commits. A further kind of reflection is added in the ontology alone, as a class, a kind concept and its rules.
