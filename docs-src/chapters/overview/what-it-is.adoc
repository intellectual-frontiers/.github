[#what-it-is]
== What an Eidolon is

A *digital reflection* is a maintained, AI-readable record of one subject: its identity, context, relationships, evidence, provenance,
history and lifecycle. It is not a replica and not necessarily a digital twin. It is enough grounded evidence for an AI to reason about
the real subject accurately and flexibly. There are two kinds, and they are not interchangeable (spec 0047):

* An *Eidolon* reflects *what exists*: a person, a company, an institution, a facility, an identifiable physical asset. Its subject
exists independently of the record.
* An *Ergon* reflects *what is created*: a product, a service, a system, a workflow, a solution. Its subject was deliberately built to
accomplish a purpose.

The concept of the Eidolon is the founder's own authored research; the system in this repository is the company's first working
instance of it, and the "Eidolon" of the next sections is that system, which holds reflections of both kinds. Ergon is its counterpart.

=== The problem

Organizations describe themselves in prose: a website, a deck, a one-pager, a founder's memory of what was decided and why. Prose
drifts. A page says the company does five things; by the time a reader, or an AI, encounters it, the company does three of them,
differently, for different reasons. A person can sense that something is stale. An AI usually cannot, and will reason confidently from
the stale version.

An Eidolon inverts the usual order. Instead of writing a description and hoping it stays true, it asserts *facts*: discrete, dated,
sourced, each carrying its own visibility. Descriptions are generated from those facts, or checked against them.

=== Specs, ontology and work

We think the best shape for any AI-native deliverable has three parts, always made in this order:

. *Specs say what must be true.* A spec is a short, numbered, testable statement of intent: "the public root MUST contain only
Public-tier facts." Because each requirement is testable, a person or an AI can check a deliverable against it instead of guessing
what the author meant. Specs live in `spec-kit/specs/`, and <<reference-specs>> lists them.
. *The ontology says what things are.* The ontology is the shared, typed vocabulary: what a Unit, a Work, a Decision, a Right, an
Audience, a Reference is, and how they relate. It reuses established standards (W3C PROV, schema.org, SKOS, Dublin Core) rather than
inventing terms, so an AI does not have to infer what "asset" or "decision" means in this company. It lives in `ontology/`.
. *Work is what gets made.* Books, websites, research records, skills, spoken works, ventures: the deliverables. Each is produced
against the specs and typed against the ontology, so it can be audited. Why does this exist, what rule shaped it, what does it
claim, who may see it.

The order is not a preference; it is a rule (spec 0001, FR-037). A new capability is established in a spec before it is represented in
the ontology, and in the ontology before it is implemented anywhere else. Each layer is only as good as the one before it: an ontology
with no spec is vocabulary without intent, and work with no ontology is prose again, ready to drift.

Why this suits AI in particular:

* *AI needs grounding, not eloquence.* A model briefed from typed facts with declared sources reasons about the company the way a
well-briefed person would. A model briefed from a brochure repeats the brochure.
* *AI needs a stopping rule.* Specs say where the work is done and where human judgment resumes (for example, where AI's role stops in
Research & IP, spec 0006).
* *AI needs to know what it may say.* Every fact declares its audience, so an AI producing a public document cannot silently pull in
something confidential.
* *People need to audit the result.* The test of an Eidolon is whether an AI reasoning from it reaches the conclusion a well-briefed
person would, and whether a person auditing it can find out why. How elegant the ontology looks does not matter.

=== What makes it hold together

* *A vocabulary before any content.* Terms are agreed before facts go in.
* *A confidentiality model that is not a ladder.* Audiences overlap (public, anyone affiliated, a specific agreement, a specific
role) and a fact may belong to several; clearance for one does not imply another.
* *A hard line between what we assert and what we merely watch.* A patent's status belongs to the patent office, not to our own
saying-so. We hold a _reference_ with a verification date and cadence, not a copy of the value.

[#two-kinds]
=== Two kinds of reflection

An Eidolon helps answer: what is this entity, what do we know about it and how do we know, what relationships does it have, what has
changed, what matters now, what should a person or an AI do next. An Ergon helps answer: why was this made, what problem is it for,
who is it for, how does it work, what does it depend on and offer, who builds, owns, operates, buys and uses it, what does it promise,
what shows that it works, and how should it evolve. Both carry the same things: a stable identifier, types, assertions with sources
and a label (observation, hypothesis, evidence, inference, recommendation or unknown), relationships with their dates, an audience,
and a steward of the record. A steward of a record does not thereby own, or have authority over, its subject.

*To decide which a new subject is*, ask four questions of the subject, never of the record, and never "is it physical, digital,
human-made or natural?", which decides nothing:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Question |It points to an Eidolon when... / an Ergon when...

|Identity
|It is an individual in the world with its own identity. / It is a design, a method or a kind of thing that has versions.

|Purpose
|It was not made for a purpose of the record's owner. / It was deliberately designed or assembled to accomplish one.

|Abstraction
|It is one particular (this device, this building, this company). / It is the design of many particulars (the model, the product).

|Lifecycle
|It exists before and apart from the record, and is observed. / It is specified, built, released and evolved.
|===

Then find the subject's class in the ontology's classification rules (`agora ontology list --match Rule`): the most specific rule
decides. If none does, or two disagree, the kind is *undetermined*: do not guess. Type the subject more specifically, add a rule
(spec first), or ask a person. A subject that is an idea, not an individual or a made thing (a theory, a hypothesis, a legal right,
a patent claim, an invention) is neither kind and gets no reflection: whether it has a kind of its own is an open question (spec 0047
OQ-1).

The boundary cases are the useful ones:

* A company is an Eidolon even though people formed it. A deployed device unit is an Eidolon; its model, its software and its
intended capabilities are Ergons, joined by `instantiates`.
* A workflow is an Ergon; each run of it is an event, not another Ergon. An AI agent's design and operating contract is an Ergon; its
running instance, identity and activity are separate subjects.
* A patent document, the right in it, the invention it describes and the product that practices it are four subjects. Who invented,
who owns, who was assigned and who may license are four relationships, and none is inferred from another.

Relationships join subjects, never records, and each has a type, a direction, a source, dates and a label for how settled it is. The
worked examples are in `spec-kit/specs/0047-digital-reflections/examples/` and are checked with the ontology: a customer account (the
company is an Eidolon, the software it bought an Ergon, and purchase, deployment, use and outcome are separate dated relationships),
a medical device (the model and the unit apart), an AI Workforce solution (design, runtime, runs and outputs apart), and rights and
inventions.

*A record typed Eidolon whose subject is something made* is still served at its identifier. `agora check ontology` (and the private
repository's check, which runs the same rules) reports it as needing review and changes nothing. A person reviews it: if it is an
Ergon, a new Ergon record is made that reflects the same subject and is derived from the old record, and the old record stays and
points to it, so identifiers and relationships do not change. A subject no one can decide stays as it is and goes on the repository's
register of open questions. Changing the rules and moving records are separate commits.
