[#intake]
== Bringing in a new idea

An intake starts when a person has an idea for something the company has no name, spec or place for yet: a new kind of work, a
product line, a line of research. The first conversation decides what the idea is called, where it lives, and who decides each part.
Work through the steps in order. Spec 0045, Elenchus works, is the worked example. It started as one sentence, a monthly compliance
audit that fails what is not working, and became a work kind, a command group and a research pillar.

=== The steps

. *Read what exists before you propose anything.* Search the specs, the ontology and the vault for the idea. Elenchus found that
the compliance specs (0028 to 0040) already held control catalogs, boundaries, an evidence ledger rule and a weekly status
report, so the new spec states only what they do not, and cites the rest.
. *Name it, and say why.* Offer several names and check each for a collision: a spec number, a product, a mark, a word that
already means something here. Use the field's own term where one exists (0019 FR-001). Elenchus uses audit words (control test,
exception, finding, plan of action) and keeps a house word only for the pattern itself. Put the reason for the name in the spec.
. *Split the idea from what carries it out.* An abstract methodology is a Noema. A deployed workflow or product that carries
it out is an Ergon, related to it by `operationalizes` (0048-noemas FR-007). Elenchus is the Noema. Whether Operational Truth® is
the Ergon is still a question for a person to answer, so the chapter asserts no relation yet.
. *Decide the kind.* A work kind names a work's primary presentation form (0015 FR-005). A new kind needs its spec, then its
`ifcore:WorkKind` individual, then a command group in the vault. Do not build ahead of that order (<<working-order>>).
. *Take the next free spec number from the current `main`.* Fetch first. Another branch may have taken the number you meant to
use. If it has, renumber yours and every reference to it before you merge.
. *Check each fact yourself.* Read the source, record what you read and when, and mark what you could not verify and what you
tried. A trademark's status comes from the registry's record and never from a literal in the ontology (0007 FR-012). A person's
statement is recorded as theirs.
. *Give a line of inquiry a research home.* A research pillar needs a stated question and a current record of what it has
established (0006 FR-002). An area groups pillars and carries no question. Put a first pillar under an existing area; promote to a
new area when sibling pillars exist. A pillar's record starts at the narrowest audience, and broadening it is a person's
Decision.
. *Leave to the person what is theirs.* Names, marks, who owns the rights, who operates the products, an audience change, and
the close of a month's work are the decision authority's (0001 FR-029). Draft them, and record the person's answer.
. *Run the checks, then integrate.* Run `./agora check`, and in the vault its own `eid check` and `eid test`. Fetch `main` and
merge. If `main` was rewritten, replay your own commits onto it. Do not merge the old history back in.
. *Record the intake.* In the vault, start from the intake record template (0002 FR-013). It states what came in, how each part
was classified, what was left out and why, and where each part entered the lifecycle.

=== Questions to settle with the person first

* What is it called, and what must it not be confused with?
* Is it a pattern, a product, or both?
* Who owns the rights, and who builds and runs it?
* Is it public, or does it start at the vault circle?
* What would count as evidence that it works, and what would count against it?
