= A quantity is a product of factors
:type: reading
:minutes: 3
:outcomes: O1, O2

Nobody has counted the piano tuners in a city of a million people, and you can still estimate how many there are.
Write the answer as a product of quantities you can reason about one at a time.

== The factors

Start with the people. A city of a million holds about 400,000 households, at two and a half people each. Perhaps
one household in twenty owns a piano, which gives 20,000 pianos. A piano is tuned about once a year, so the city
needs 20,000 tunings a year.

Now the tuners. A tuner finishes about four pianos a day and works about 250 days a year, which comes to 1,000
tunings each. Divide the tunings the city needs by the tunings one tuner can do, and the city supports about 20
tuners.

== Why the errors cancel

Each factor is uncertain, and some of your guesses run high while others run low. Across four or five factors the
errors tend to offset each other, so the product lands within a factor of ten more often than any single guess
would suggest. The method works because you state each assumption where a reader can see it and change it.

== What to write down

For every factor, write the number and the reason for it in one line. A reader who disagrees with a factor can then
replace it with their own and recompute the answer. An estimate without its assumptions is only a number, and no one
can check it. The https://example.org/estimation[worked examples on estimation] show more of these.
