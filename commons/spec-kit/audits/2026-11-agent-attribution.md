## D-2: the attribution model of agent-authored commits

- **Decision:** the human of record takes responsibility for a commit that an agent makes under D-1. The commit is authored and committed as Shahid Shah with the GitHub no-reply address, because that is who answers for it, and it discloses the agent and the session in its own message: the trailer `Co-Authored-By` names the agent and the trailer `Claude-Session` gives the session's address, and the line `Refs #N` or `Closes #N` names the ticket. No agent adds `Signed-off-by`: that tag states that a person has read and accepts the change, and only a person says it (the convention of the Linux kernel's guide for coding assistants, which asks for an `Assisted-by` trailer from the agent and leaves `Signed-off-by` to the human).
- **The trailer:** the kernel's `Assisted-by: AGENT_NAME:MODEL_VERSION` was considered. The `Co-Authored-By` trailer that `repoctl commit` already writes carries the same disclosure (the agent and its model), so no new trailer is added and the commit tooling is unchanged. The identity check `E-HYGIENE-IDENTITY` and the held merges of `commit.heldMerges` stay as they are; history is never rewritten.
- **Where an agent is the author:** only where a person expressly authorizes an agent to be the author of that commit in its message.

## D-3: what this arrangement does not give

- **It is not an electronic signature in the sense of 21 CFR Part 11.** A signature of this file says who decided and when only because an agent wrote it on the owner's statement; it does not show a signer's unique identity or its two components, and its meaning is stated by the text and not by a signing system. A regulated customer would need signed commits or tags (for example with a hardware key) and an audit trail that the signer can be tied to.
- **History can be rewritten until branch protection is set,** which is postponed to December 2026. A commit's author is a name that anyone with push rights can give; the hygiene check finds a merge by another identity and does not prove who made a commit.
- **The human of record does not read each commit.** The responsibility is taken under a standing authorization, with the scope and the exclusions of D-1, and the checks of the repository (`repoctl check`, the tests, the proof register) are what stand between an agent's commit and the owner's name.
- **Nothing here is validated** as a computerized system of a quality system; no regulator's text that was read addresses an agent's working notes, and what regulators require of records (audit trail, access control, validation, change control, signatures with their meaning) is given here by Git, the specifications, the register and these Decisions only to the extent each of them gives it.
