## Preface

I built Intellectual Frontiers to find and compound Native Alpha. Everything
else is a means to that end.

I wrote this doctrine for the people who work with me, invest alongside me,
build companies with me, and eventually run Intellectual Frontiers without
me. These are my convictions about how we see the world, make decisions, and
protect the company. Use them, question them, and hold me to them.

I have spent more than three decades in founder mode. I have started
companies, built products, hired teams, faced customers, served on boards,
invested my own money, supported mergers and acquisitions, worked inside
large companies, and watched good ideas fail for ordinary reasons. I learned
that a clever thesis is cheap. A company that can really work is rare.

I built Intellectual Frontiers to turn those lessons into a repeatable way of
working. I am not trying to preserve every old habit. I am trying to preserve
the judgment underneath the habits: find the unusual advantage, test it
honestly, make a decision, and compound what proves real.

Intellectual Frontiers is not a lone venture. I set the doctrine and retain
the decisions only I can make. We carry out the work through a team of
employees, fractional leaders, specialists, operating partners, advisers,
founders, and investors. We give people real responsibility and expect them
to challenge my assumptions when the evidence calls for it.

The work concentrates where I have spent my career: healthcare delivery and
the technology around it, and the modernization of government systems. Those
are the markets where I can see the real workflow, and where an unusual
advantage is worth something.

**Rule of interpretation:** when this document says what I believe or expect,
treat it as the default. Change the method when evidence earns a change. Do
not dilute the governing idea for convenience.

## 1. Order of operations

| Step | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Find | What is unusually true here? |
| Prove | What small hard thing earns a scarce commitment? |
| Decide | What will we do now? |
| Compound | How does use make the advantage stronger? |

## 2. What I believe

- **Advantage is usually already present.** It may sit in a founder,
  workflow, relationship, dataset, patent, market position, distribution
  channel, reputation, or operating capability. Our first job is to see it
  clearly.
- **Evidence must cost somebody something.** Compliments and broad interest
  are easy. Money, staff time, implementation access, data, reputation,
  changed behavior, and real decision dates are harder to fake.
- **A decision is the point of the work.** Research, prototypes, patents,
  publications, and meetings matter only when they help us choose what to
  build, fund, publish, license, sell, partner on, or stop.
- **Small tests beat large plans.** We would rather learn one hard fact in
  four weeks than admire a polished plan for four months.
- **We choose hard things on purpose.** Easy work tells us little. We start
  with one small thing that is genuinely hard to do. If we can do it and the
  evidence matters, we earn the right to take on a larger, harder problem.
- **Stopping is part of building.** A cheap, honest no protects time, money,
  reputation, and attention for the work that deserves them.
- **Companies must be able to stand on their own.** A Studio, investor,
  adviser, service firm, founder, or vendor should not become a permanent
  hostage point.
- **Plain speech is a management tool.** If we cannot explain the customer,
  advantage, evidence, risk, and next decision in ordinary language, we
  probably do not understand them yet.
- **AI raises the standard for judgment.** When building software gets
  cheaper, deciding what deserves to exist becomes more important, not less.

**What I care about:** Better decisions. Faster proof. Clean rights. Stronger
relationships. Independent companies. Less fixed overhead. Honest stopping.

## 3. Native Alpha governs

**Definition:** Native Alpha is an unusual and disproportionate advantage
that already exists and is strong enough to change a real decision.

- **Native** — we start with what is already true about a founder, company,
  asset, workflow, relationship, or market position. We do not invent a
  story that makes an ordinary idea sound special.
- **Alpha** — not novelty or enthusiasm. The advantage changes what becomes
  possible and gives us a reason to act that a capable competitor cannot
  easily buy, copy, or recreate. Native Alpha does not make hard work easy.
  It gives us a credible way to do hard work that others cannot readily do.

**Restated:** based on who you are, what you know, what you control, and
where you sit, Native Alpha is something you can see, know, decide, or do
that others cannot readily reproduce, and it changes what is possible.

**Tests:**

| Test | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Already there | An unusual advantage exists before we tell a story about it. |
| Costly proof | Someone commits money, time, access, data, reputation, or changed behavior. |
| Real decision | We choose to build, fund, publish, license, partner, sell, or stop. |

If the evidence does not change a decision, we still have a hypothesis.

**Warning:** a patent can be a signal. A famous founder can be a signal. A
large market can be a signal. None of them is proof by itself.

**Where alpha lives:**

| Source | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Founder or team | What can these people see, learn, recruit, or earn faster than others? |
| Customer workflow | What can we observe because we sit inside the real work? |
| Rights, assets, and data | What do we control and may lawfully use to create a useful option? |
| Trust and distribution | Who gives us access, candor, authority, or reach that strangers do not receive? |
| Operating know-how | What have we learned by doing the hard work repeatedly? |

## 4. Native Alpha and first principles

First-principles thinking asks what must be true. It breaks an opportunity
into the customer, workflow, money, technology, rules, incentives, and human
behavior underneath it. It helps us separate a real constraint from a habit
that the market stopped questioning years ago.

Native Alpha asks a different question: what is unusually true here? Sound
reasoning can tell us that a problem is solvable. It cannot tell us why this
particular founder or company deserves to win. That is why Native Alpha
stays in charge.

**Restated:** first principles asks what must be true. Native Alpha asks what
is unusually true here. We ask whether that unusual truth is strong enough to
change a decision.

First principles helps us:
- Name the real customer and job
- Expose assumptions about rules, cost, technology, timing, and behavior
- Design the smallest test that can prove us wrong
- Show where the advantage meets the real problem

First principles, misapplied, hurts us:
- Creates confidence before customers commit
- Treats trust, history, distribution, and reputation as clutter
- Produces an answer people will not adopt, buy, approve, or operate
- Rewards clever analysis while hiding weak rights, demand, or execution

**Rule:** reason from the ground up, but do not throw away the ground we
already earned.

## 5. Each unit has one job

| Unit | Job |
| --- | --- |
| IP | Run the research and protect and commercialize what it produces |
| Press | Understand markets and explain the truth clearly |
| Capital | Put money behind evidence with discipline |
| Studios | Build the companies that should exist |
| Network | Find and prove the people who can carry the work |

Full charters for each unit are drafted separately in
[`context/units/`](../../context/units) — see `ip.md`, `press.md`,
`capital.md`, `studios.md`, and `network.md`.

**Unit boundary (do not confuse a signal for proof):** a patent does not
force an investment. A publication does not prove demand. Studio work does
not earn automatic funding. An investment does not buy a founder's voice. An
introduction is not a reference.

## 6. How an opportunity moves

| Stage | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| Signal | Patent, operator insight, research, customer workflow, market change |
| Thesis | Native Alpha hypothesis, ownership, mandate, conflict map |
| Proof | Design partner, experiment, implementation, paid evidence |
| Decision | Continue, pivot, stop, publish, license, invest, build |
| Compound | Each use strengthens rights, data, trust, distribution, or economics |

Evidence can enter through any unit. Authority, rights, and economics must
remain explicit at every handoff. A signal may start with a patent,
manuscript, customer complaint, workflow observation, market change, research
program, operating relationship, portfolio result, or a person Network
found. We record where it came from, who owns it, what is confidential, who
has authority, and what conflicts may exist before the work becomes
expensive.

A patent does not prove demand. A publication does not prove a market.
Studio activity does not justify investment. Investment does not prove
product-market fit.

**Decision packet:** advantage, demand, rights, economics, governance, next
proof.

**Handoff rule:** every handoff must name the legal party, beneficial owner,
decision-maker, rights, economics, evidence, and conflict process. Trust and
common ownership do not remove that work.

**Search before build:** the question after a promising thesis is never
"what company should we build." It is whether someone else — a founder
Network surfaces, or a company already in the market — is already pursuing
this thesis credibly. If their evidence is as strong as ours or stronger,
backing them is a better use of Capital than building a competitor
ourselves. Studios is a last-mile capability: it exists for when that
search comes up empty and the evidence still justifies spending our own
money and time to find out whether we are right. A thesis originating
inside Intellectual Frontiers earns us nothing by itself — if a better
founder already exists, backing them is the more intelligent expression of
our own thesis.

**The system circulates, it does not assemble:** IP does not hand
something to Press, which hands it to Capital, which hands it to Studios.
A customer conversation inside Studios can destroy an assumption IP made.
A Press publication can surface a customer segment nobody was looking for.
An LP conversation can reveal the venture structure is wrong. A founder
search can reveal that the real bottleneck is rarer talent, not weaker
technology — or that someone else is already better positioned than we
are. Every discovery, from any unit, can revise what another unit already
believed. Nothing here is a one-way handoff.

**Evidence, not product, is what moves:** a patent produces IP evidence. A
publication produces audience evidence. A customer experiment produces
demand evidence. A product produces behavioral evidence. A sale produces
economic evidence. An LP conversation produces capital-market evidence. A
founder search produces human-capital evidence. A partnership produces
distribution evidence. Even a properly designed failure produces evidence.
All of it feeds back into the same Native Alpha thesis — nothing our units
produce is the end of the line.

**The decision checkpoint:** at every stage, the honest options are the
same five: continue, change the thesis, back someone else, build it
ourselves, or stop. Back someone else is checked before build it
ourselves, not after — never because it is our only remaining option, but
because it is usually the cheaper way to compound the same Native Alpha.

## 7. A small core, a flexible bench

**The parent owns:**
- The Native Alpha method, thesis history, and decision standards.
- Company strategy, business-unit mandates, and major continue or stop
  decisions.
- Rights stewardship, chain of title, and asset records at the parent level.
- Capital-allocation authority, conflicts, governance, and LP relationships.
- Venture Architecture, partner selection, relationship memory, and portable
  records.

**Rented, not owned:** engineering, design, research production, recruiting,
sales execution, administration, fund operations, legal, tax, audit,
valuation, specialized regulation, and other repeatable work should stay
outside the core until steady use, strategic value, decision sensitivity,
economics, and runway justify a full-time hire.

**One shared-services layer, not five:** legal, finance and accounting,
compliance, and general operations are routed to independent, specialized
providers the same way for every unit — the pattern Capital already runs
for fund administration, legal, tax, audit, and valuation is not a
Capital-specific arrangement, it is what every unit does. No unit builds
its own internal legal, finance, or compliance team while another unit
rents the same function. A function graduates to an internal hire only
when the hiring rule below is met for that function specifically, not
because one unit happened to reach for it first.

**Hiring rule:** do not turn temporary work into permanent payroll. Hire
full-time only when the work is steady, strategically important, financially
sound, and supported by at least 18 months of runway.

**Delegation:** I retain the final decision on allocation of investment
capital. We give every other recurring responsibility a named owner, written
authority, a measurable output, and a clear escalation point. Delegation is
real only when people know what they may decide and when they must bring the
question back.

## 8. AI makes evidence cheaper

AI can compress research, analysis, engineering, administration,
documentation, experiments, product work, customer discovery, sales
preparation, reporting, and learning. That lets a small team test more
precisely and stop weak ideas earlier.

AI also makes software construction less scarce. That raises the bar. A
company cannot claim Native Alpha because it has access to the same models
and tools as everyone else. The advantage may sit in workflow position,
lawful data rights, trust, customer access, distribution, know-how, IP, or an
operating loop that AI makes stronger.

**Uses:**
- Turn a vague observation into a falsifiable Native Alpha claim and a list
  of reasons it may be wrong.
- Find patterns and hidden constraints in workflows, customer evidence,
  research, and rights records.
- Build prototypes, test harnesses, documentation, and operating records fast
  enough to support narrow experiments.
- Compare our claims with usage, economics, implementation evidence, and
  actual results.
- Keep decision logs, experiment records, rights maps, runbooks, and
  institutional memory current.

**Rule:** use AI to make evidence cheaper and judgment better. Do not use it
to make weak ideas look busy. This applies to this repository too: an AI
agent drafting or editing specs here should cite its source and be willing to
say what it does not know, per the claims standard in
[`context/company.md`](../../context/company.md).

## 9. How we judge progress

| We look for | Not this |
| --- | --- |
| Decision quality: we name assumptions, contrary evidence, authority, and the next decision | More meetings or longer memos |
| Small hard proof: one bounded, difficult test that can change a real decision before we attempt something larger | Easy demos, broad plans, or more AI projects |
| Early stopping: we stop weak work before pride and sunk cost take over | A large backlog of active ideas |
| Usable rights: clean title, useful scope, clear duties, financeable terms | Patent or application count |
| Paid proof: customers commit money, workflow change, data, implementation, renewal, or reputation | Friendly pilots or generic letters of intent |
| Compounding relationships: each engagement produces better access, trust, reference behavior, or sourcing | Contact counts or event attendance |
| Independence: a company owns its roadmap, records, customers, systems, rights, and governance | Studio headcount or hours |
| Operating discipline: capability grows while fixed overhead grows more slowly | More employees, providers, funds, or entities |

**Before every experiment:** write down the belief, the smallest test, the
budget, the decision date, the evidence required to continue, and the
evidence that will cause us to stop. Do this before seeing the result.

## 10. When we stop

Stop when any of these hold:
- The claimed advantage is common, purchasable, or easy to copy.
- The pain is real but not urgent, funded, or reachable.
- A design partner will not commit access, data, staff time, authority,
  payment, or a decision path.
- The rights are unavailable, encumbered, irrelevant, or hard to finance.
- The company depends on a founder, provider, channel, or relationship that
  cannot become portable.
- Repeatable delivery and revenue remain harder than the thesis allows.
- The opportunity conflicts with a mandate, duty, nonprofit boundary, or
  another vehicle.
- The next dollar or month buys activity but no decision-relevant fact.

**Failing well:** a failed experiment can still be productive when it fails
cheaply and leaves behind useful learning, relationships, methods, research,
data rights, technical parts, IP, or a clearer reason not to continue.
Keeping a weak idea alive to protect pride is failure.

## 11. What we will not become

| Not this | Because |
| --- | --- |
| A patent holding company | Rights matter only when they create usable commercial options |
| A promotional house organ | Press must teach and clarify. It must not manufacture approval |
| A claim of institutional tenure | Capital is formally new, even though my operating, angel, board, M&A, and exit experience is not |
| A conventional venture studio | Studios exists to answer hard questions and make companies independent, not to collect permanent service relationships |
| A clean-sheet philosophy | First principles must not erase trust, rights, history, relationships, distribution, or know-how |
| A consulting conglomerate | Outside partners perform bounded work. The parent owns judgment, rights stewardship, allocation, and governance |
| A rolodex | Network's output is evidence about people, not a list of names or a favor economy |
| An AI story | AI amplifies real advantages. It is not the reason a company deserves to win |
| A capital deployment machine | Capital's job is judgment about evidence, not a target for dollars placed |

## 12. Closing

Find what is unusually true. Prove it with a commitment that matters. Make a
decision. Then earn the right to do something larger and harder.

Do not protect my old answers just because they are mine. Protect the method
that produced them. If better evidence changes an answer, change it. Record
why. If the governing idea itself needs to change, make that choice openly
and explain what new evidence made the old doctrine insufficient.

I have been operating in founder mode for most of my career. Intellectual
Frontiers is where that experience becomes a company-building and investment
system. I want the people who come after me to make it better, but I do not
want them to make it vague.

Intellectual Frontiers turns unusual thoughts into evidence, and evidence
into decisions. We do not need every thought to become a company. We do not
even need every good company to be one we started. We need every good
thought to find its best possible form.

**Final test:** Native Alpha is real only when it changes what we choose to
do.

**When unsure, ask three questions:** What is unusually true here? What
evidence would change our mind? What is the smallest hard thing we can do to
get that evidence? Then decide.
