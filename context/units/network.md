**Job:** Find and prove the people who can carry the work.

**Color:** Network Indigo `#4A4A8C`.

**The question Network asks:** Who has unusual knowledge, capabilities,
relationships, reputation, experience, or access relevant to an opportunity,
and how do we find them when needed without building another static resume
database?

## Why Network exists

Network is the newest unit and the one the original doctrine did not have. It
exists because the hardest constraint on the firm is rarely capital or
rights — it is finding the small number of people who can actually carry a
piece of work, and knowing enough about them to stake a decision on it.

The unit works the way the rest of the firm works. A search starts with a
written thesis: what the work is, what would have to be true about a person
to do it, and what evidence would change the firm's mind. Signals come from
public work, shipped systems, writing, code, filings, and the operating
record — not from a job title. What comes back is an evidence packet on a
small number of people, with what is known, what is inferred, and what is
missing stated separately.

Relationship memory is the compounding asset: three decades of operating
relationships in healthcare and government technology, held as a record of
who did what and how it turned out, so the next search starts further along
than the last one. Ask Network is how that record gets queried in plain
language.

Counts published about a search are illustrative unless the packet says
otherwise — and the packet says so in the same breath.

## The rule

An introduction is not a reference, and a reference is not evidence.
Network's output is an evidence packet that can change a hiring or
partnering decision, with the gaps named.

**Evidence Network produces:** search theses, signals, evidence packets,
relationship memory, and acted-on introductions.

**What Network must never become:** a rolodex. Its output is evidence about
people, not a list of names or a favor economy — see the constitution's
"what we will not become" section.

## How a search runs

Most search starts with a query and ends with a list, which puts the
judgment on the reader. Network does the judging first: a person reads
every packet, decides who is worth contacting, and answers for that
decision in writing. When the search ends, the outcome is compared to the
thesis, and the next search starts from the difference.

Twelve stages, in order:

| Stage | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Thesis | Someone writes down who would have to exist for a piece of work to succeed, and what would make that person unusual. |
| 2. Discovery | Network looks outward at what is already public. The internet is the database; it is not copied into one. |
| 3. Surfaced | A small set of candidates comes back, each attached to the thesis it answers. |
| 4. Evidence | Every candidate gets a packet: what was found, where it came from, and what is still unknown. |
| 5. Human judgment | A person reads the packet and decides whether to reach out. No score decides this. |
| 6. Contact | One specific message about one specific thing, sent by a person who can act on the answer. |
| 7. Conversation | What is learned in the conversation is written down — it's the part the internet doesn't have. |
| 8. Validation | The hypothesis about what this person is unusually good at either holds up or it does not. |
| 9. Match | The person is connected to the hunt, venture, fund, or editorial project that needed them. |
| 10. Experiment | The match starts as a small, bounded piece of real work, not a title. |
| 11. Outcome | The experiment produced something or it did not, and Network records which. |
| 12. Learning | The thesis is corrected with what actually happened; the next search starts from what this one got wrong. |

A failed search is still useful — it says what the thesis got wrong. The
second search for a similar person is almost always better than the
first.

## Doctrine

- **Write the thesis before looking.** Deciding who is being looked for
  comes before looking. The written thesis is the work; candidates come
  after.
- **Treat every signal as a clue.** A talk, a filing, a roster — each is a
  reason to look closer. None proves anything by itself.
- **Native Alpha is a hypothesis.** It holds only after a conversation or
  a small piece of real work says it does.
- **Always explain why someone surfaced.** If the sentence can't be
  written, they should not have come up.
- **Five strong candidates beat five hundred results.** A long list
  usually means nobody did the thinking.
- **A relationship beats a profile.** What someone says directly is worth
  more than anything they've published.
- **An outcome beats engagement.** The only score kept is whether real
  work happened.
- **Every hunt improves the next.** The next search starts from what this
  one got wrong.

## What Network measures

**Refuses to measure:** profiles created, registrations, page views,
messages sent.

**Measures instead:** hunts with a written thesis, candidates a person
judged worth contacting, conversations that changed the thesis,
experiments started, and outcomes — including the ones that failed.

## The evidence packet

Every candidate a search surfaces gets a packet, not a profile. A packet
states: who the person is and their role; what hunt they were surfaced
for and why, specifically; the signals that led to them; the Native Alpha
hypothesis about what makes them unusual and why it matters; the actual
evidence for each claim, with its source; the relationship path to them,
if any; the unknowns, named as unknowns; the next action; a confidence
level (Low, Medium, High); and a status. Nothing in a packet is presented
as more certain than the evidence supports — an unknown stays listed as
an unknown rather than being quietly dropped once a candidate looks
promising.

A hunt — the search a packet belongs to — states its own thesis, the
Native Alpha it's looking for, what qualifies and disqualifies a
candidate, the signals it watches for, its running counts (surfaced,
reviewed, in conversation, experiments), and what it has already learned
and corrected.
