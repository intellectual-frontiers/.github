**Job:** Build the companies that should exist.

**Color:** Signal Teal `#1F7775`.

**The question Studios asks:** What venture should we create to exploit this
advantage, and what is the cheapest credible path to proving whether it
deserves to exist?

**Purpose:** Decide what company should exist, prove it with the least
waste, and make it independent when it earns the right.

## Where the practice comes from

Studios formalizes work done since founding Netspective in 2001, including
co-founding Physia, simplifyMD, Citus Health, Influential Networks,
HealthcareGuy, HITSphere, Unblock Health, HealthIMPACT, and other ventures.
Physia's intellectual-property assets were acquired by COMSYS. simplifyMD
merged with Azalea Health. Citus Health was acquired by ResMed.

That was not watched from a distance — it was done as a founding technologist
and operating executive: building products and teams, working through
healthcare regulation, supporting customers, and living with the results of
product and capital decisions, plus time as an adviser, Entrepreneur in
Residence, CTO, CISO, CSO, and fractional executive. That range taught how to
enter a company in the middle, find the real advantage, separate facts from
inherited habits, make progress, and leave behind a company that does not
depend on the operator.

Customer-led engineering and forward-deployed work keep Studios close to
reality. A Studio must not become a custom consulting shop: the discipline is
to observe the real workflow, find the narrow repeated problem, and learn
which part can become a product or a scalable service.

## Disciplines

| Discipline | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Venture Architecture | The work of deciding what should exist, which Native Alpha it uses, what must be true, who should care, which rights matter, and what proof earns more time or money |
| Venture Architect | The person who inventories Native Alpha, applies first principles, frames the thesis, designs tests, recruits design partners, assembles help, and drives a clear decision |
| Venture Development Lifecycle | The path from observation and thesis through customer validation, experiments, paid proof, repeatability, and independence |

## Lifecycle

**Observe** (a real asymmetry) → **Frame** (Native Alpha thesis) →
**Validate** (buyer and workflow) → **Experiment** (smallest credible test) →
**Paid proof** (costly customer signal) → **Repeat** (economics and delivery)
→ **Independence** (company can stand alone)

Every stage ends in a decision gate: continue, pivot, stop, finance, license,
sell, or spin out. The next dollar or month must buy evidence, not momentum
theater.

## Steps a Venture Architect works through

1. Name the source, owner, confidentiality, mandate, and possible conflicts.
2. Inventory the unusual advantage already present. State the Native Alpha
   thesis in plain language.
3. Break the opportunity into customer, workflow, money, technology, rules,
   incentives, and human behavior.
4. Separate real constraints from inherited convention. Do not discard
   trust, rights, history, distribution, or know-how.
5. Ask what a capable competitor could reproduce and how quickly.
6. Choose the smallest hard thing that can prove us wrong. Seek a costly
   signal.
7. Write the continue and stop conditions before seeing the result.
8. Choose: continue, revise, narrow, publish, license, finance, sell, spin
   out, partner, or stop.

**Build rule:** start with one small thing that is genuinely hard to do.
Finish it, measure it, and learn from it. Only then take on something larger
and harder.

## What counts as a design partner

| Requirement | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Workflow access | People can observe the real work |
| Executive sponsor | Someone can authorize change |
| Customer design partner | A real operating partner contributes something scarce |
| Data or implementation | The environment is real, not a demo |
| Payment or decision path | There is a credible route to a commercial yes |

## What counts as evidence — and what it isn't proof of yet

| Evidence | What it shows | What it is not proof of |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Workflow access | We can see the real task, handoffs, constraints, and exceptions | A right to reuse confidential know-how or data |
| Executive sponsor | Someone can authorize staff time, security review, and change | A buyer or budget by itself |
| Data commitment | We can test with representative data under defined rules | Ownership or permission to train unrelated models |
| Paid proof | The customer assigns money to a bounded result | Repeatable revenue or product-market fit |
| Reference behavior | The customer will stake reputation on the result | Renewal, margin, or scalable delivery |

**Independence test:** could a qualified founder, board, investor, or
replacement provider continue this company tomorrow using company-controlled
records, rights, systems, and relationships? If not, the company is not
independent yet.

## Two kinds of company

Studios' portfolio holds two different kinds of thing, each with its own
obligations, plus a third category shared services sit in.

**A venture** is chartered as a company, not a project: its own entity,
its own operator, a licensed rights position, and a closure condition
written down before the first hire.

**Software** is code as a work, not a company: some of it is open source
and public, some stays inside a venture, and each entry records its
license and status rather than implying one. There's no entity or
operator requirement — the obligation is transparency about license and
status. When something built for internal use turns out to be worth more
outside, it gets spun out rather than kept as an internal tool.

A **shared service** sits between the two: built to stand alone, usable
by every venture in the portfolio, and spun out if outside demand for it
is strong enough.

## Shared services

Studios also runs shared services across the portfolio, so a company does
not rebuild what the firm already knows how to do.

Demand Engineering runs when there is no proven audience yet; its outcome is
a named audience, a claim that survives contact with them, and a first step
they will actually take. Revenue Engineering runs after that, as a closed
loop from market definition through the human sales handoff and back into
learning. There is a handoff gate between them rather than a gradual blur —
the specs, signals, scorecards, and evidence carry over instead of being
rebuilt. If they do not hold up, that is a result too, and it is cheaper to
learn there than in a pipeline that never converts.

Both run on a small human core with an AI harness, and a service is named for
the outcome it owns. Demand Engineering and Revenue Engineering are each
trademarked as an "AI Workforce" product, and that name is a claim, not a
label: if humans still do most of the work, or several developers have to
stay in the daily production loop, the service is called AI First or AI
Augmented instead — regardless of how it's branded.

Reproducible science work sits alongside them, keeping the research and the
code that supports it verifiable by someone outside the firm. Its concrete
form is the **Companion Executable Source Code Repositories** service:
pairing a published paper with a containerized code, data, and
documentation repository that keeps updating — a complement to peer
review, not a replacement for it. As of this writing it is at the ideation
stage, not a running service yet, and should be described as such
wherever it's mentioned.

IPLG (Intellectual Product-Led Growth) is the framework behind how these
services, and Studios' other output, turn into something a visitor can
use directly rather than only read about — the same framework
`context/units/ip.md` describes for IF IP's research. Studios' own
version of it names the "executable actions" a visitor can take per unit
(for IF Studios, things like turning a page into a Venture Architecture
exercise, or becoming a design partner). Like everywhere else, it's held
to the Native Alpha test: the AI underneath it is not the advantage; the
compounding system built around it might be.

## Evidence Studios produces

Committed design partners, paid proof, repeatable demand, independent
governance, and financeability.

**Boundary:** Studio activity does not justify investment — see the
constitution's unit-boundary rule.
